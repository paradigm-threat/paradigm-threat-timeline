## FB-001 — Fomenko “state sponsorship” framing; catastrophism vs. New Chronology

### Raw feedback (anonymized)

> I quickly skimmed through the Russian translation of the book, skimming individual passages. A complete course in world history, spanning 336 pages. I hadn't imagined such a thing was possible.
>
> I initially considered noting the passages where the New Chronology materials were inaccurately retold, but decided there was no point. I'll cite just one passage from Chapter 2:
>
> "In our time, the state-sponsored historian Anatoly Fomenko..."
>
> The assertion that A.T. Fomenko is supported and sponsored by the state is untrue. He is a recognized mathematician, and in 1994 he was elected a full member of the Russian Academy of Sciences. However, all works on history and chronology, written first alone and, since the 1980s, in collaboration with G.V. Nosovsky, are categorically rejected by professional Russian historians. Their opinions can very well be considered the opinion of the state. During the Soviet era, when the CPSU Central Committee held supreme power, official historians weren't above filing denunciations of Fomenko to party officials. Fortunately, these authorities didn't persecute the authors of the New Chronology. Apparently, they deemed their hypotheses harmless to communist dogma. But they also offered no support. Nothing has changed in the post-Soviet era. As far as I know, the authors of the New Chronology never received any government grants.
>
> I'll touch on some of your assertions that have nothing to do with the New Chronology. Many of them are linked to the theory of catastrophism, which lacks convincing evidence. In Chapter 3:
>
> "The first date that can be reliably recorded is 4077 BC. In that year, energy flowing through the system caused the planets to rotate like electric motors."
>
> The veracity of this date, or the event itself, is uncertain. But that's just the tip of the iceberg. Then there are tales of Saturn, then giants, and even Martians. I'm having trouble deciding what genre this falls under. Fantasy? Esotericism? But it's definitely not science.
>
> What was the point of writing this book, which required the author to invest so much time? I suspect the main goal was to discredit the New Chronology. If a scientific theory is diluted with tales of Martians and other esoteric nonsense, readers will not trust it. As the Russian proverb goes, a fly in the ointment spoils a barrel of honey.

### Investigation (what can be checked)

**1. “State-sponsored historian” (Fomenko)**

- **In-repo sources of the phrase (both corrected):** The same sentence appeared in paradigm-threat-files [`history/chronology/page.md`](https://github.com/clevertree/paradigm-threat-files/blob/master/history/chronology/page.md) and in the timeline book source [`content/00.overview/00.02.00-background.md`](https://github.com/clevertree/paradigm-threat-timeline/blob/main/content/00.overview/00.02.00-background.md) (overview *Background*). The Russian book translation cited by the reviewer likely inherited or paralleled that framing. **Action:** Replace “state-sponsored” with wording that matches **institutional facts** below.
- **Academy membership vs. sponsorship of New Chronology:** English Wikipedia, *Anatoly Fomenko*, states he is a **full member (Academician) of the Russian Academy of Sciences (1994)** for **mathematics**, lists a **State Prize of the Russian Federation (1996)** for **mathematics**, and separately notes that **most Russian scientists and worldwide historians** treat his **historical** work as **pseudoscientific or antiscientific**. That combination supports the reviewer’s core point: **RAS standing does not equal state sponsorship of New Chronology**, and **professional historians in Russia** (often treated as the “official” voice) **reject** the chronology project.
- **Eastern / RAS-sourced criticism (not endorsement of NC):** The same Wikipedia article references the RAS digest **“In defence of science”** (*В защиту науки*), PDF bulletins on [ras.ru](http://www.ras.ru/digest/fdigestlist/bulletin.aspx)—**Russian-language, academy-issued** material in the genre of **defending scientific standards against pseudoscience**. Useful when the timeline or site needs a **non-Western** citation for “academic Russia distances itself from Fomenko’s **history**,” distinct from lazy “Kremlin puppet” tropes.
- **Denunciations / no grants / no persecution:** The reviewer’s Soviet/post-Soviet narrative (denunciations, hypothesis treated as harmless, **no** grants) is **not fully verified in this note**; it is **plausible** given known **hostility** from **professional historians** and **silence** on NC in **official** history curricula. **Gap:** primary archival citations (party files, grant ledgers) still welcome in `OUTSTANDING_QUESTIONS` or a dedicated investigation file.

**2. 4077 BC, Saturn, giants, “Martians,” genre**

- **Perception:** The reviewer collapses **Saturnian / electric-universe / catastrophist** lines (Jno Cook / Talbott-class material, etc.) with **Fomenko’s New Chronology** and reads the **mix** as **contamination**—intentional or not—of a **statistical chronology** thesis with **esoteric** content.
- **Timeline posture (already in rules):** `content/` presents **multiple calendar systems** (Scaligerian, Fomenko, Saturnian, etc.); not all claims are presented as **mainstream science**. **Application:** Where book or site prose **bundles** NC with **cosmology** without a **bright label**, Eastern (and Western) readers who **accept** NC **only** as a **historical** hypothesis will **bounce**. **Remedy:** Explicit **chapter or section framing**: “Alternate cosmology / catastrophism (not part of Fomenko’s core method)” vs. “New Chronology (dynastic / statistical).” Address **“Martians”** literally: if the text means **Mars as planet / mythic role** vs. **extraterrestrial beings**, one sentence of disambiguation reduces **bad-faith** and **honest** confusion alike.
- **Intent (“discredit NC”):** Unfalsifiable as motive; **falsifiable** as **effect**. If mixed-genre presentation **predictably** triggers the proverb the reviewer quoted, **structure** is a bug regardless of author intent.

### Author reply (English, recorded for the record)

Thank you very much—this is exactly the kind of feedback I need. It's important for me to understand where I went wrong and how exactly I formulated my thoughts in a way that misrepresents them, so I can dig into the sources myself, document the citation, or, if there's no citation, honestly leave the investigation open. Many threads in this book will remain open even after publication—for me, that's a normal working principle, not "everything has already been proven."

Regarding the first point (Fomenko), you're right: the wording about a "state historian" was inaccurate and offensive in its meaning. Fomenko is a recognized mathematician, an academician of the Russian Academy of Sciences (including since 1994) in mathematics, and his work on the "new chronology" is rejected by the professional historical school in Russia and elsewhere. I've taken this into account and updated the text (including the timeline support) to avoid the impression of state sponsorship of a chronological project. Thank you for pointing this out so specifically.

Regarding the second point (4077 BC, Saturn, "Martians," etc.). Here, I don't have the kind of proof you seem to mean by science—and, frankly, neither does the "new chronology" in the eyes of its critics: what some call proof, others consider methodologically unsound; what some call faith, others consider a hypothesis or even a "working model." I don't pretend that catastrophism and the Saturnian line look like textbook physics to you. I didn't include a separate point specifically on Saturnian cosmology in the open questions document because your text didn't contain a new, verifiable claim (reference, fact, counterargument to a specific source)—rather, a general assessment of the genre ("fantasy," "esotericism"). This is the assessment I heard; as soon as I have any concrete information that can be used in the investigation, either quoted or refuted, I'll add it—or else leave the trail unanswered if the archives are empty.

Why this book at all? Briefly: I'm a programmer. Almost every "official" or mass narrative I've encountered in my life has seemed painfully improbable, contradictory, cynical, and boring to me—and for a long time, I've had a growing suspicion that there's some kind of coherent story beneath that layer. This book isn't a sudden hobby project, but the culmination of about ten years of reading and analysis, recently greatly accelerated by agent-based research tools. To your question, "Why write if it undermines trust in the New Chronology?" I'll answer this: I'd love to have a collection like this at any point in my life—one where everything is structured through causality and traces, not through what the reader is supposed to "simply believe." If the conflation of Fomenko's chronology with the Saturnian branch seems like a fly in the ointment to you, that's a fair signal about presentation and framing; I'm working to make the boundaries between the different frameworks (statistical chronology vs. alternative cosmology) more clearly visible, without abandoning the fact that for me, there is one unified picture of the world.

Thanks again for your time and precision in your wording—that's rare and a great help.

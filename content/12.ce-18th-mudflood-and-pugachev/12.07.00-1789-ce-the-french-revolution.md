# The French Revolution

> **TL;DR:** 1789–99 **prefigures** French imperial capacity—church and feudal assets **societized**, mass **levée**, meritocratic officer corps—mirroring Britain’s earlier consolidation and **prerequisite** for **Napoleon’s** 1812 crusade machinery.

The French Revolution (1789–1799) — like the British Civil War before it — **predated** the country's major imperial expansion and provided the institutional groundwork for it. The revolution accomplished a societized consolidation of resources and political reorganization that replaced divine-right monarchy with the template for modern Western governance.

## The Pattern: Revolution Before Empire

| Country | Revolution | Imperial Expansion |
|---------|------------|-------------------|
| **Britain** | Civil War (1642–1651); Glorious Revolution (1688) | British Empire peak (18th–19th c.) |
| **France** | French Revolution (1789–1799) | Napoleonic Empire; later colonial empire |

In both cases, the revolution reorganized the state *before* imperial expansion: centralized taxation, standardized administration, meritocratic military, and ideological mobilization.

## Societized Consolidation

The Revolution transferred resources from Crown, Church, and nobility to institutions claiming to represent "the nation":

- **Church lands nationalized** (November 1789) — sold to create loyal proprietors
- **Feudalism abolished** (August 4, 1789) — seigneurial dues, tithes, privileges ended
- **Levée en masse** (1793) — total mobilization; citizens became soldiers

This is the same process the British Civil War pioneered: Crown/Church assets redirected to Parliament's merchant-finance coalition.

## The Four Pillars of Western Governance

The Revolution produced the political template the West still uses:

1. **Meritocracy** — *La carrière ouverte aux talents*. Napoleon rose from artillery officer to Emperor. Competence replaced birthright.
2. **Plutocracy** — Property qualifications for voting (Constitution of 1791). Wealth as gate to power.
3. **Particracy** — Rule by faction: Jacobins, Girondins, Thermidorians. Party structure as governance.
4. **Electoral Autocracy** — Strong executive, weak legislature, popular mandate via plebiscite. Napoleon's coups ratified by referendum.

## The Direct Line to 1812

The Revolution produced the conditions for Napoleon's 1812 invasion of Russia — the first Reverse Crusade. A centralized, ideologically motivated state with mass conscription and meritocratic officers was the prerequisite for marching an army east. Without the Revolution, there is no Napoleonic Wars.

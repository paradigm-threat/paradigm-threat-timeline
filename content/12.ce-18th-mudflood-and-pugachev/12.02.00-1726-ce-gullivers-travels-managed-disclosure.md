# 1726 CE: *Gulliver's Travels* — travel testimony and the satire shell

> **TL;DR:** **Conclusion:** *Gulliver* (1726) is an **early nonfiction → fiction** reclassification under **satire**, from a writer under **financial pressure**—same **managed-disclosure** family as later Mars literature. **Brobdingnag** + **Great Tartary** language **mirror** Rus–Horde / gunpowder chronology **in parallel only**; **Laputa** states **two Martian moons** ~**151 years** before Hall—detail lives in the Swift investigation files, not duplicated here.

The first edition presented *Travels into Several Remote Nations of the World* as a factual travel account by a ship’s surgeon. The same documentary frame appears across a wider set of titles in the [fiction-presented-as-fact survey](/influence/predictive_programming/fiction_encoding/fiction-presented-as-fact-investigation.md). When the book was absorbed into the “classic satire” shelf, the mechanism looks like the later library **fiction** quarantine described in [Mars literature as reclassified nonfiction](/timeline/evt-mars-literature-as-reclassified-nonfiction)—only earlier, and under a humor label rather than a card-catalog rule.

Swift was under serious **financial pressure** in the mid-1720s; the publication context is summarized with citations in [Swift — Mars, giants, publication stance](/influence/predictive_programming/fiction_encoding/swift/swift-mars-giants-publication-stance.md) and [debate rails and drift](/influence/predictive_programming/fiction_encoding/swift/swift-investigation-debate-rails-and-drift.md). This timeline does not need to repeat that ledger; it notes the pattern—another skilled writer needing money—because it matches the Burroughs/Tolkien parallel discussed in the predictive-programming appendix.

## Brobdingnag, “Great Tartary,” and the gunpowder backdating

In Part II the giant king treats European **cannon and powder** as a late, barbarous refinement and sketches a chronology in which those arts appear **hundreds of years** after they had already matured in the Brobdingnagian scale of states. That arithmetic sits on the same investigative lane as Fomenko’s **Rus–Horde** compression and “hidden early powder” readings; the Swift-specific parallels (including “Great Tartary” language and reflection of a **Russo-Russian** imperial order without fixing a single battle name) are collected in [Swift — Tartary / Rus timeline mirrors](/influence/predictive_programming/fiction_encoding/swift/swift-tartary-rus-timeline-mirrors.md).

## Laputa: two small bodies close to Mars

Part III’s flying-island astronomers describe **two tiny moons** of Mars with short periods and tight orbits—mapping, in the investigation file, onto **Phobos** and **Deimos** well before Asaph Hall’s telescopic identification in **1877** (about a century and a half later). Orbital numbers, source passages, and coincidence arguments stay in [Swift — Mars, giants, publication stance](/influence/predictive_programming/fiction_encoding/swift/swift-mars-giants-publication-stance.md) so this entry does not duplicate the Mars math.

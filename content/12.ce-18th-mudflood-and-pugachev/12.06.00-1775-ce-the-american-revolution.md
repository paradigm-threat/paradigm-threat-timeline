# The American Revolution

> **TL;DR:** The war is the **Atlantic eastern jaw** of the **Pugachev** partition (Fomenko); **Bank of England** debt drives colonial extraction; **French** loans link straight to **1789**; independence preserves slavery while **Hamilton** clones British national debt.

The American Revolution (1775–1783) was not an isolated colonial revolt. It was a small piece of a much larger Atlantic realignment — one node in a network of British debt, French intervention, Dutch finance, and the final fracturing of the Hordian remnants in North America.

## The Eastern Front of the Pugachev War

Fomenko's New Chronology identifies the American War of Independence as the **eastern front** of the same conflict that produced the Pugachev Rebellion:

> *"'The War of independence' in North America was the struggle with the weakening Russian Horde. The Romanovs attacked the Horde from the West — and from the East in America it was attacked by the Americans 'fighting for independence'."*
> — Fomenko, [*History: Fiction or Science?* Ch.8 §1](https://chronologia.org/en/how_it_was/08.html)

Pugachev's defeat (1774–1775) coincided with — and enabled — the American colonies' seizure of Hordian territory in the continent. The "thirteen colonies" were the eastern theatre; Moscow Tartary's collapse was the western.

## The Bank of England Connection

The revolution cannot be understood without the London Financial Coup (1664–1694). The Bank of England and its debt architecture required colonial taxation to service. The entire sequence — Stamp Act (1765), Townshend Acts, Tea Act — was debt extraction by a financial system barely seventy years old. The colonists were not rejecting "tyranny" in the abstract; they were resisting a specific financial apparatus.

France bankrolled the American war with loans and military support, which directly triggered the French fiscal crisis that became the French Revolution (1789). The two revolutions are linked by capital flows — and by the same pattern of societized consolidation documented in the British Civil War investigation.

## What the Revolution Did Not Achieve

- **Slavery:** Preserved and constitutionalized
- **Indigenous sovereignty:** Accelerated westward expansion; British treaties (Proclamation of 1763) voided
- **Financial independence:** The new republic was immediately indebted to French and Dutch creditors; Hamilton's assumption of state debts (1790) created a national debt structure modeled on Britain's

The revolution replaced one debt master with another. The Bank of England's template — central bank, national debt, parliamentary management — was reproduced by Hamilton within seven years of independence.

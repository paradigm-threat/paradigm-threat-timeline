# Paschal Calendar Fixed — Council of Nicaea Redated

> **TL;DR:** Church tradition says the **First Council of Nicaea (325 CE)** fixed Easter rules and the **Nicene Creed**; **Fomenko–Nosovskiy** argue the **paschal calendar (Computus)** could not have been canonized before **~784**, fits astronomical full moons best around **877**, and was later **backdated to 325**—so the famous council is primarily a **calendar anchor myth**, not a verified 4th-century theological congress. **[Gregorian reform (1582)](/timeline/evt-gregorian-calendar-makes-slight)** sells continuity from that phantom date.

## What the conventional story claims

The [Council of Nicaea](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/christianity-nicene-creed-first-council-of-nicaea) (Iznik, Turkey, **325 CE**) is taught as the moment when hundreds of bishops, under **Constantine**, settled Christ’s relation to the Father (*homoousios* vs *homoiousios*), produced the **Nicene Creed**, and standardized **when Easter is calculated**. Modern coverage still treats it as one of Christianity’s foundational political-theological assemblies.

On this timeline we **do not deny** that bishops disputed doctrine or that a creed lineage exists. We follow **G.V. Nosovskiy’s calendrical argument** (summarized in Fomenko’s *How It Was In Reality*, [Ch.7 §34](https://chronologia.org/en/how_it_was/07_34.html)): the **Computus** attached to “Nicaea” is astronomically **misdated** if placed in the 4th century.

## Fomenko–Nosovskiy: what actually got fixed, and when

The paschal calendar has two parts:

1. **Immovable** — Julian civil months (including leap years).
2. **Moveable** — Easter and dependent feasts, computed from **vernal full moon** and **Sunday** rules.

Nosovskiy’s independent dating uses those rules as an astronomical clock:

| Constraint | Implication |
|------------|-------------|
| Easter must **not** coincide with Jewish Passover | After **784 CE**, Christian Easter and Passover **never** coincide again; a 4th-century council could not have canonized rules that were **violated eight times** in the 300s (including **326**). |
| Paschal “full moons” must track observed astronomy | Reasonable match only **c. 700–1000 CE**; best alignment at the start of the **13th Great Indiction — 877 CE**. |
| Earliest defensible bound | Not before **784**; likely **10th–11th century**; church tradition of **Matthew Vlastar (14th c.)** implies not before **743**. |

**Placement on this timeline:** **877 CE** — the astronomical optimum for fixing Computus — in the [11th-century reset](/timeline/evt-1053-ce-11th-century-ce-common-era-begins) when Fomenko also places the **first durable written calendar**, cities, and *Almagest*-class astronomy. Canonization may have stretched into the **11th–14th centuries**; Scaliger later **projected** the event to **325**.

## Not the same as the “Empire of Nicaea” (1204)

**Do not conflate** this entry with the **Nicaean Empire** after the **1204** Crusader sack of Constantinople — Theodore Laskaris, Iznik as exile capital, and Rus–Horde successor politics ([13th-century chapter](/timeline/evt-1300-ce-the-russian-horde-tartarian-empire-e)). Same toponym (**Nicaea / Iznik**), different layer: **calendar myth** vs **medieval state formation**.

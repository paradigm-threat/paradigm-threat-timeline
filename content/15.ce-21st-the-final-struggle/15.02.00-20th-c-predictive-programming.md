# 20th C. Predictive Programming

![Florence Deeks vs H.G. Wells — The Spinster and the Prophet](/media/13.ce-19th-rise-of-communism/wells-spinster.png)

> **TL;DR:** After Mockingbird, fiction scaled a mirror trick to predictive programming: bury inconvenient history under copyright “fantasy” so later witnesses sound like plot recaps. Wells through Lovecraft, Tolkien, Goldman, Asimov, and Card supply the literary layer; film, TV, and games widen it—and whether echoes count as evidence depends on which chronology sits in the background.

The decades following [Operation Mockingbird](/timeline/evt-operation-mockingbird-historical-chain) saw the industrialisation of a technique far older than the CIA: reframing real events as fiction. From the 1890s onward, authors—some conscious operatives, some cultural carriers—systematically converted suppressed history into “fantasy” and “science fiction.” The effect was erasure. Once a real event becomes a novel, anyone who later discovers the truth sounds as if they are describing a plot summary.

This is the backward direction of [predictive programming](/timeline/evt-predictive-programming-film-tv-games): not announcing future operations, but burying past ones under copyright-protected fiction.

Comparisons between encoded fiction and “real history” usually assume the conventional chronology. If that timeline is wrong, shifted, or partly synthetic, control events will not line up with narrative echoes: mismatches then look like coincidence or overreading rather than failed proof. Mass redaction and chronology fights mean the public record is a thin witness; fiction may still carry displaced residue of what was erased, while rigorous demonstration stays blocked until the comparator calendar is questioned or rebuilt. On this line of work, a working prediction holds that scattered investigations—each fragile under official dates—could converge toward harder-to-dismiss patterns once a reconstructed or alternate timeline is accepted as the baseline for comparison, not as automatic fact but as the yardstick. H. G. Wells set an early template with *The Crystal Egg* (scrying), *The War of the Worlds* (heat ray, red weed), and the Plattner and Davidson stories (dimensional and remote-viewing motifs). He was likely compromised: reputation sabotage and unprofitable fiction for Macmillan point toward serving powerful interests; see [compromised court cases](/influence/predictive_programming/fiction_encoding/compromised-court-cases-declared-reality-investigation.md). Arthur Machen’s *The Great God Pan* tied brain surgery to dimensional perception; *The Angels of Mons* showed fiction passing as news. Algernon Blackwood’s *The Willows* and *John Silence* treated thought as a surveillance surface. H. P. Lovecraft wrote roughly 104 stories that encode scrying, remote viewing, and telepathic broadcast. Edgar Rice Burroughs’s Barsoom offered polar conspiracies and telepathic technology. J. R. R. Tolkien used the Red Book as pseudotranslation, assembled *The Silmarillion* as systematic reworking of older matter, and left copyright that runs until 2043 and beyond.

William Goldman’s *The Princess Bride* (1973) belongs to a later phase: the Morgenstern frame, the father’s oral cuts and “good parts” abridgement (redaction narrated as fiction), and the mask dialogue about everyone wearing them “in the near future.” By then the Second World War was long past, and public memory of the sharper fights over myth-making and documentary-framed fiction associated with Wells and Tolkien had thinned. A working conclusion ties Goldman-style success to institutional favors and mass-publishing gatekeeping. A further working conclusion (very likely on strategic logic, unproven bibliographically) treats S. Morgenstern as probably a real or once-real authorial line whose complete edition could no longer surface, which would have made the abridgement gambit safe and opened space for historic fantasy as repackaged copyright fiction.

That strategic picture raises a mechanism question: ordinary obscurity rarely justifies confidence that every edition of a book has vanished. Earlier, in May 1933, Nazi Germany staged massive public book burnings—iconic events across German cities, including Berlin’s Bebelplatz—that destroyed large quantities of targeted works in the open; a hypothetical Morgenstern line in German libraries or trade could have disappeared there too ([Nazi book burnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_book_burnings)). One historically documented class of mass removals after the war is the purging of defeated-regime literature—German denazification is the familiar label. In Italy, Allied Order No. 4 (signed 13 May 1946) ordered removal and destruction of fascist, militarist, and Nazi literature from libraries, schools, and bookstores—administrative book destruction, not slow market fade. Other former Axis zones underwent parallel Allied and successor controls that could strip whole categories of print from legal trade and public shelves. A working hypothesis (no list naming Morgenstern has been located in the linked investigation) holds that a real author line later tagged as “S. Morgenstern” might have been caught in such a sweep, so that by the late 1960s or early 1970s whoever cleared the frame could gamble that no checkable original would reappear—making a fictional Morgenstern abridgement effectively non-falsifiable in public catalogues (“perfect crime” shape in publishing terms). See [Princess Bride investigation](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/predictive_programming/fiction_encoding/princess-bride/index-princess-bride-andronicus-fezzik-investigation.md) **Key findings §5** (coda) and section *Book destruction (1933, 1946) and the “perfect crime” window*.

The same frame assumes the audience shares a child’s impatience: kingdoms, customs, reasons, and Morgenstern’s satiric history are boring unless cut to action. The reader is aligned with the boy who wants entertainment, not inquiry; torture, court politics, and war are delivered as thrills rather than as material that belongs in open historical judgment. A working reading holds that this posture models mass audience as anti-curious—amusement over substance—while the “dull” layers are exactly where redaction can hide. See the Goldman subsection in [Predictive Programming: Literature — Extended](/timeline/evt-predictive-programming-literature), the [full Princess Bride investigation](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/predictive_programming/fiction_encoding/princess-bride/index-princess-bride-andronicus-fezzik-investigation.md) (**Key findings §6**; section *Anti-intellectual frame: reader as the child who skips the real book*). Orson Scott Card’s *Ender’s Game* (1985) put planetary destruction inside “simulations” that were real battles and landed on the U.S. Marine Corps Reading List.

## 1942–1992 CE — Asimov: Marx in Space!

Isaac Asimov’s Three Laws of Robotics read as slave codes, and the Foundation cycle retells Marx in space. For the full analysis, see [Asimov: Three Laws and Marx Encoding — Extended](/timeline/evt-asimov-three-laws-and-marx-encoding).

Each author in this cluster converts suppressed knowledge into protected fiction. Wells in the 1890s exemplifies Hordian erasure; Lovecraft in the interwar years fits Mockingbird consolidation; Tolkien marks a phase of formalised literary rights; Card sits near the Cold War peak. For extended author-by-author evidence, see [Predictive Programming: Literature — Extended](/timeline/evt-predictive-programming-literature).

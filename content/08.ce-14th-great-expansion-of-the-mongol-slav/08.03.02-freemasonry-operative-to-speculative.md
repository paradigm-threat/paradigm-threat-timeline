Mainstream Freemasonry admits uncertainty about its origin. The oldest English document usually cited — the **Regius Poem** (manuscript ~1390) — is already a *copy* of older material on masonry as a craft under royal charter. The central mythos — Solomon's Temple, the murdered master architect **Hiram Abiff**, sacred geometry — reads in this chronology as **distilled memory** of monumental construction under a single imperial authority; Fomenko places that authority in the Rus-Horde Empire (see [The Masons](/timeline/evt-the-masons)).

## Operative Masonry Under Empire

Operative masons were the hands that executed the XIV–XVI century building programme: geopolymer cement, megalithic fitting, geometry, logistics across continents. Italian architects [summoned to build Moscow](https://chronologia.org/en/how_it_was/06_35.html) as the Second Jerusalem were reportedly never allowed to leave — *"all three architects could not leave Moscow and had to lay their bones there."* That pattern matches **itinerant initiated builders** bound by oaths. The legend of Hiram Abiff — killed for refusing to betray craft secrets — is read here as **memory** of those builders, later abstracted into ritual.

## The Reformation and the Break from the Khan

Fomenko treats the Reformation not primarily as protest against the Pope but as Western governors **breaking from the Hordian Empire** ([chronologia.org Ch. 6 §19](https://chronologia.org/en/how_it_was/06_19.html)). When the empire fragmented, the builder class was **annihilated or absorbed**. Survivors' knowledge could no longer be exercised as open craft guild work at imperial scale.

## From Operative to Speculative

Operative Masons became **speculative** "Freemasons": lodges retained signs, tools, and oaths while **losing** the material technology. Polytheistic and imperial references thinned into prayer to the **GAOTU** (Great Architect of the Universe) — preserving form while hollowing content. Compass and square became **symbols**; the chemical and logistical secrets passed into alchemical garble or were lost (see [Imperial Technology & Knowledge](/timeline/evt-imperial-technology-knowledge)).

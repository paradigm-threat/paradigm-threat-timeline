# Prisca sapientia — ancient wisdom as epistemic foundation

> **TL;DR:** **Prisca sapientia** (“ancient wisdom”) is the working assumption of this timeline and of Paradigm Threat research: the ancients held a vast, coherent understanding of universe, nature, and theology that was later lost, redacted, or captured—not replaced by an automatic upgrade called “modern science.”

![Prisca sapientia — ancient wisdom as epistemic foundation](/media/00.overview/prisca_sapientia.png)

The phrase is Latin, but the instinct is older than Rome. Cultures that still had living ties to pyramid builders, weather doctors, and sky witnesses did not treat their elders as quaint. They treated memory as a **library that could burn**. When the library burned, what remained in fragments was called myth, superstition, or folklore so the replacement curriculum could install without debate.

**Prisca sapientia** names that inversion explicitly. It does not mean every clay tablet is infallible. It means the **burden of proof** tilts: when a medieval chronologist, a Flexner report, or a textbook chapter disagrees with a global pattern in ancient testimony, degradation and capture are **live hypotheses**—not crankery by default.

## Recovery, not nostalgia

The goal is not to cosplay the past. No one needs a costume empire. The goal is to **recover** usable truth modernity buried: how pyramids functioned as shelter and hospital geometry, how disease was read as terrain and season before germ theory became a license, how chronology compresses when duplicate dynasties are folded, how cosmology once placed Saturn above the pole before the sky was declared stable for all time.

Recovery uses every honest tool—Fomenko’s statistical chronology, Morozov’s comparative catastrophe reading, anticontagion human experiments from Desgenettes to twentieth-century cold trials, electric-universe plasma mechanics, indigenous creation dates that refuse geologic deep-time without discussion. The tools disagree on details; they agree that **something older was smarter than the story we were given**.

## Anti–chronological snobbery

C. S. Lewis called **chronological snobbery** the assumption that the latest intellectual fashion is the truest. Industrial medicine, state schooling, and peer-review cartels behave as if novelty were virtue. A drug approved yesterday outranks a hospital stone that stood four millennia; a paper published this quarter outranks a calendar reformed after a sky event.

**Prisca sapientia** inverts the default. New work must **earn** trust against the ancient baseline—not dismiss it with a label. That is why this timeline places [Background](/timeline/evt-background) chronology critics beside [Core Concepts](/timeline/evt-core-concepts) sky claims: both are attempts to **read** what survived, not to **worship** a date on a museum placard.

## How this timeline uses the assumption

Every chapter band—Golden Age, Dark Ages, Rus–Horde empire, mudflood, twentieth-century break—can be read as episodes in a **single loss curve**. Institutions that gained archives gained the right to **shorten** memory. Institutions that gained laboratories gained the right to **meter** healing. Institutions that gained broadcast gained the right to **synchronize** fear.

The [Third Story](/timeline/evt-the-third-story) argued mythology and rational science are **both** incomplete without electric-cosmic testimony. **Prisca sapientia** is the **ethical** complement: even when physics is argued in public, the **moral** default remains that ancients were not fools and moderns are not automatic priests of truth.

Concrete threads already on this timeline that exemplify the stance include compressed chronology and phantom time ([Timeline synchronization](/timeline/evt-timeline-synchronization)), Saturnian cosmology and changing year length ([Core Concepts](/timeline/evt-core-concepts)), and the fracture of the eastern Christian-recognized empire remembered as Prester John and Tartary ([Background](/timeline/evt-background)). The Paradigm Threat files site holds the same foundation in long form: [Prisca sapientia (reference)](https://paradigmthreat.net/docs/PRISCA_SAPIENTIA.md).

## What this does not claim

**Prisca sapientia** is not a blanket license to treat every meme as gospel. Investigations still separate **documented**, **author thesis**, and **speculative** tiers. It is an **epistemic direction**: when old and new clash, ask who benefited from the clash before you award the win to the newer voice.

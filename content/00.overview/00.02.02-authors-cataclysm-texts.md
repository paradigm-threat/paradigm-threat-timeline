# Authors Who Read Ancient Sources as World Cataclysm

A network of researchers across physics, plasma science, mythology, geology, and independent scholarship argues that ancient myth, scripture, sacred art, and archaeology are not allegory but eyewitness records of real planetary events and cosmic disasters within the last 8,000 years. The foundational work is Velikovsky's *Worlds in Collision* (1950), which demonstrated that Venus and Mars had recent close encounters with Earth — recorded in Exodus, Joshua, Homer, and Chinese annals. De Santillana and von Dechend's *Hamlet's Mill* (1969) showed that myth worldwide encodes precise astronomical knowledge, not primitive fantasy. These two books opened the door for everything that followed.

The Saturnian school — Talbott (*The Saturn Myth*), Cardona (*God Star* series), and Jno Cook (*Recovering the Lost World*) — reconstructed the specific polar configuration: Saturn, Venus, and Mars aligned on Earth's north polar axis, visible as the Wheel of Heaven. Thornhill and Juergens provided the physics: the Electric Universe model in which gravity is secondary to electromagnetic forces, stars are externally powered by galactic currents, and planetary orbits change through electrical interaction. Peratt confirmed the plasma connection by matching petroglyphs worldwide to laboratory Z-pinch plasma instabilities — proving ancient humans recorded what they saw in the sky, not what they imagined. Van der Sluijs catalogued the world-column and axis-mundi traditions that independently preserve the polar configuration memory.

On the geological and archaeological side, Schoch re-dated the Sphinx to ~10,000 BCE using water-erosion evidence and proposed a solar plasma catastrophe. Hancock built the case for a pre-Ice Age civilization destroyed by the Younger Dryas impact. Heinsohn compressed the medieval timeline using stratigraphy, showing "ghost empires" counted from the same archaeological layer. Ginenthal rebutted Sagan's dismissal of Velikovsky and investigated mammoth extinction as evidence of sudden catastrophe. Cremo documented suppressed archaeological evidence of extreme human antiquity. What unites all of them is the refusal to treat ancient sources as fiction — and the convergence of their independent methods on the same conclusion: the sky was different, the planets were closer, and civilization remembers.

# 1463 CE — Man-eating griffins and the steppe extinction campaign

> **TL;DR:** **1463 CE** (this chronology) is when literate Europe still recorded, through **Greek excerptors**, a **steppe panic**: peoples in flight from **man-eating griffins** “from the ocean.” After the **Russian imperial center** was **broken and memory-routed**, coordinated **hunting and bounty culture** drove the last populations to **extinction**; **physical evidence** was **broken up** into **museums and private collections**, on the same pattern argued for **giant** remains.

In **conventional chronology**, a similar chain appears in **Priscus** (fragment **30**), dated to about **463 CE**: the **Saraguri** attack the **Acatiri** after a cascade of displacements ending in peoples fleeing **man-eating griffins coming from the ocean**. Mainstream scholarship reads that line as **fabulous migration folklore** or **Herodotean echo**, not zoology — see the discussion in Maenchen-Helfen, *The World of the Huns* (UC Press, 1973), ch. **IX · Language**, esp. **p. 436** in the standard edition.

On **this timeline**, that excerpt is treated as a **misdated shard** of a **15th-century** crisis. By **1463 CE**, the **Rus-Horde** world order that had anchored Eurasian security was already **shattered** and **re-narrated**; what survived in Greek and Latin collections was **scrap**, **misplaced centuries**, and **moralized** barbarian history. In that window, **humans** were not debating whether griffins were “real” in a modern taxonomic sense — they were **killing**, **trapping**, and **driving** dangerous **apex populations** off the steppe and littoral corridors, using the same **military–commercial** networks that later **partitioned** odd skeletons, **bronze zoomorphs**, and **trophy** pieces.

## After the Russian kingdom: memory, hunt, disappearance

Once the **Russian kingdom** (in the timeline’s sense: the **imperial Horde–Orthodox** complex later slandered as “Tartary” in Western propaganda) was **defeated in story** and **administratively forgotten**, there was no **protected** category for **non-human** megafauna that had lived alongside it. **Griffins** — whether understood as **composite predators**, **exaggerated** coastal raiders, or **something else** the sources could only name from **myth** — entered the same pipeline as other **inconvenient** biology: **extirpation**, then **silence**.

The **evidence trail** did not vanish into thin air. It **fractionated**: **bones** and **artifacts** labeled as **Scythian**, **Sarmatian**, or **Oriental** **curios**; **metalwork** with **griffin-like** inlays sold as **art**; **manuscript** lines left to **classicists** as **literary topoi**. That pattern parallels what this project tracks for **human giants**: remains **removed** from **open** burial contexts, **re-housed** in **museums**, **private cabinets**, and **closed** **collections**, while **public narrative** collapses to **fable**. See [Giants of the Rus-Horde](/timeline/evt-the-giants-of-the-rus-horde) and the open investigation [Myth fauna in sober texts — human extirpation?](/history/chronology/investigations/myth-creatures-textual-record-human-extirpation-investigation.md) on paradigm-threat-files.

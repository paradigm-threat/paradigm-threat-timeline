The most detailed chronological reconstruction of Saturnian events with specific calendar dates derived from cross-cultural ancient sources. ## What Cook Contributed That Others Did Not

Cook's unique contribution is **cross-cultural calendar triangulation** — applying Mesoamerican Long Count data, Chinese annals, and Mesopotamian king lists simultaneously against the mythological sequence to produce specific calendar-year dates, rather than the relative sequences Talbott established.

This timeline's event dates in the Golden Age and Dark Ages sections come primarily from Cook's calculations:
- **4077 BCE** — start of the Saturnian polar configuration (Saturn connects to the inner solar system)
- **3147 BCE** — end of the Golden Age, catastrophic breakup
- **685 BCE** — stabilization of Venus and Earth's current orbits (from Rose & Vaughan's Venus Tablets analysis)

## Differences from This Timeline's View

Aside from differences regarding the "before time" (creation epoch and pre–4077 BCE) that are noted elsewhere in this timeline, I disagree with Cook on a particular passage in his [Chapter 2: A Synopsis](https://www.saturniancosmology.org/syn.php). Cook writes of the 1492 BCE Venus contact:

> "Venus again made an electric contact with Earth, causing a crushing repulsive blow in the east Central Pacific. The Pacific islands were wiped clean of any trace of humans, except for the petroglyphs carved on every island thousands of years earlier. Coastal South America and Central America were inundated with water, **leaving sea-water traces in lakes high up in the Andes**, and possibly causing a sudden rise in the coastal range of the Andes by thousands of feet. The blow was followed by an electric arc traveling through the Pacific, the Indian Ocean, and part of India…"

Cook does not study alternate modern history (e.g. the [MudFlood / MFEE](/timeline/evt-mudflood-evidence) hypothesis or Fomenko-style compression of recent centuries). He therefore tries to explain how the Andes formed within a conventional deep-time geological and ancient-historical framework, which rules out the possibility that the Andes or their present elevation could have arisen much more recently. In that context he cites salt-water traces high in the Andes as evidence of the 1492 BCE inundation. I could not find the study he relies on (his Note 5 is empty in the mirrored copy), and in any case I do not see why a single repulsive blow and inundation as he describes it would leave lasting salt water in mountain lakes, since there were no further deluges to maintain or replenish such a signal. The claim remains an open question for this timeline.

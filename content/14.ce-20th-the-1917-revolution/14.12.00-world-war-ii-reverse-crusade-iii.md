### Did Hitler Even Write *Mein Kampf*?

The authorship of *Mein Kampf* is an open question. Hitler allegedly dictated portions to Rudolf Hess at Landsberg Prison (1924), with Emil Maurice assisting in early transcription. No surviving original manuscript in Hitler's hand exists. Hess transported the manuscript to Berlin for approval and had full editorial access. The *Zweites Buch* (1928) — Hitler's "second book" on foreign policy — was suppressed by Hitler himself and kept secret until 1945, raising questions about consistency of literary voice. When the puppet's handlers control the text, the puppet's "authorship" is beside the point.

## The Firebombing: What the West Actually Did

While the Eastern Front consumed 80% of German military capacity — with the Soviet Union suffering **27 million dead** to break the Wehrmacht — the Western Allies' principal contribution was not a liberation campaign but a **systematic firebombing of German and Japanese civilian centres**:

- **Dresden** (February 1945): A city with no military significance, packed with refugees fleeing the Eastern Front. Allied bombers dropped incendiaries that created a firestorm visible from 100 miles away. Phosphorus clung to human bodies, turning people into living torches. The city had no anti-aircraft defences, no searchlights, nothing.
- **67 Japanese cities** were area-bombed with napalm before Japan surrendered. Tokyo alone lost 100,000 civilians in a single night (March 10, 1945) when 279 B-29s dropped 1,665 tons of napalm. 270,000 buildings destroyed.
- Total: the US firebombing campaign destroyed **180 square miles** across 67 cities, killed over **300,000**, and injured 400,000 — not counting Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

The incendiary campaigns targeted architecture, infrastructure, and civilian populations — not military targets. The Allies announced a policy of "precision bombing against military targets" while conducting area saturation of entire cities. Ancient buildings, churches, libraries, homes — centuries of irreplaceable European and Japanese civilization — burned in hours.

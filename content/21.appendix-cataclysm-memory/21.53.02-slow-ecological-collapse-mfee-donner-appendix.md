# Slow ecological collapse — MFEE through the Donner era

This appendix summarizes an **open investigation** in paradigm-threat-files: a **possible** decades-long biological aftershock after the acute [MudFlood Energetic Event](/timeline/evt-the-mudflood-world-cataclysm) (~1774 CE), running into the **1840s–50s**.

The thesis is **not** that every disaster of the period was natural. It asks whether **trophic collapse** could **lag** the mud burial: soil biota and invertebrates first, then birds and game, then crops and herds, with **humans** lasting longest on **stored food, rationing, and consolidation**. **Cannibalism** (e.g. [Donner Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donner_Party), 1846–47) is treated as a **terminal** phase after other options fail. **Trail of Tears**, **orphan trains**, and the **Irish Great Famine** appear as **correlates** — the last also readable as **genocide-by-logistics** if food routes were closed during late stress.

The Donner episode is often bracketed with the Forlorn Hope (1846–47), the snowshoe party that split off to fetch help and left a short, well-documented trail of starvation, killing, and cannibalism among the dead. The Lost ’49ers (1849), seeking a shortcut to the diggings, were driven into the Death Valley country and died chiefly of thirst and exposure until outside relief arrived—a parallel “wrong turn” disaster without the Sierra snow narrative. Many 1840s–50s trains that fell behind and met early winter in the Sierra or Rockies starved on a smaller scale; cannibalism at sea under famine (e.g. *Essex*, 1820) shows the same last resort predating the overland gold rush.

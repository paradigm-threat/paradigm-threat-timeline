## There Was Never One Communism

The 19th century produced not one "communism" but a *war* between radically different visions. Marx's version won — not on merit, but through **mass-distribution via controlled publication networks** while alternatives reached very few.

**All communisms head down one of two roads:** dictatorship/fascism (vanguard becomes the new ruling class) or republicanism (revolutionary energy matures into self-governance). Most 19th-century socialists reached for the second road. Marx's version was designed to make that transition impossible.

Organic communism produces self-governing communities owing nothing to international finance. The Pyramidal Empire needed communism on **their terms**: violent, centralised, atheist, administered by an infiltratable party apparatus. That is what Marxism was for.

**Ivan Germanovich Kinkel** (1883–1945) proved — without intending to — that communism required no revolution, no vanguard party, and no destruction of existing culture.

### What Kinkel Discovered

In 1921 Kinkel published *"Attempt at Constructing a New Theory of Economic Development of Cultural Mankind"* (312 pages). Where Marx proposed linear progression (primitive communism → slavery → feudalism → capitalism → communism), Kinkel demonstrated development operates on **two simultaneous levels**:

**Level 1: Three Great Cultural Cycles** — Egyptian-Babylonian-Peruvian (~5000–550 BCE), Greek-Roman (~1800 BCE–5th c. CE), and European-Slavic (Medieval onward).

**Level 2: Four Recurring Economic Forms** — (1) communist/communal, (2) petty-individual, (3) capitalist, (4) state-collectivist.

Critical discovery: **Form 1 — communism — naturally recurs at every civilizational cycle's beginning.** Development moves as a **spiral**, not a line.

### Why This Was Intolerable

Kinkel's model was lethal to Marxism: communism is *natural* (Form 1 appeared without manifestos); violent revolution was a *feature*, not a bug, designed to destroy institutions and knowledge; Marx's economics was deliberately reductive; capitalism was merely Form 3, recurring in every civilization — transitioning away required no apocalyptic violence.

Kinkel proposed the **biogenetic law of social development**: newer civilizations recapitulate older economic forms before advancing — the opposite of dialectical materialism.

## Gottfried Kinkel: The Road Not Taken

**Johann Gottfried Kinkel** (1815–1882) — poet, art historian, Bonn professor — unlike Marx, actually *fought* in 1848. Wounded, arrested, sentenced to life. Carl Schurz broke him out of Spandau; **Dickens** campaigned for his release. Schurz became U.S. Senator — showing what 1848 revolutionaries *could* have become.

![Gottfried Kinkel's Escape from Spandau](/media/13.ce-19th-rise-of-communism/gottfried-kinkel-escape.png)

Kinkel opposed Marx on the fundamental question: *act now, or wait for "conditions to ripen"?* Kinkel said: build republic through courage and democratic action. Marx despised this. Kinkel's wife Johanna — composer, journalist, revolutionary — fell or threw herself from a window in London, 1858. Tombstone: *Freiheit, Liebe und Dichtung* — "Freedom, Love, and Poetry."

## The Suppression

Kinkel published under pen names **M. Mladenov** and **A. Fridyung** to avoid reprisal. Communist historians branded his models *"kinkeliadi"* and quarantined his work for half a century. He was rejected by every bloc: Soviets (denied Marx), Fascists (exposed pseudo-economics), Western liberals (predicted capitalism's end).

His fate matched a systematic pattern: **Simeon Demostenov**, **Sergei Bulgakov** (exiled 1922), **Peter Struve** (exiled, died in poverty), **Nikolai Kondratiev** (executed by Stalin). Independent economic thinkers silenced across all ideologies. Had Kinkel's work circulated at Marx's scale, revolutionaries might have had an alternative preserving spiritual and cultural life — without destroying every church and document connecting Russia to its Hordian past.

# The US Civil War

> **TL;DR:** The war is read as **British-balanced** division of a rival power; **slavery** functions as **mobilization lever**, not sole cause; the sudden **1865** collapse pairs with a **Verne / penetrator** disclosure thesis (DU-class ordnance) in the linked weapons article.

The US Civil War (1861–1865) is conventionally framed as a moral crusade against slavery. The timeline framework identifies a different structure: **British involvement on both sides**, **slavery as the instrument of war rather than its cause**, and the possible deployment of **advanced weapons** that ended the conflict.

## British Funding of Both Sides

England funded and supplied both Union and Confederacy:

- **Confederate side:** Liverpool blockade runners supplied arms and equipment; the *CSS Alabama* and *CSS Shenandoah* were built in British shipyards, violating neutrality
- **Union side:** British banks and investors lent to both parties; cotton-finance links to Lancashire incentivized Southern support while Northern bonds sold in London
- **Diplomatic leverage:** The Emancipation Proclamation was partly aimed at blocking British recognition of the Confederacy by reframing the war as anti-slavery

A prolonged, divided America reduced competition for the British Empire. This is the same pattern: external powers profiting from regional division, keeping potential rivals weak.

## Slavery as Instrument, Not Cause

Slavery was not the *cause* of the war — it was the *instrument* that waged it:

- **Emancipation (1863)** was a military measure: deprive the South of labor, recruit black soldiers, block British recognition
- **~180,000 black soldiers** served in the Union army (US Colored Troops). Northern freedmen, struck by severe poverty after being detached from their economic base, were an easy recruiting pool
- **Black regiments** were assigned disproportionately dangerous roles (Fort Wagner, Petersburg) with higher casualty rates

The Union weaponized emancipation and black recruitment to field an army and win. "Slavery as cause" was amplified to justify total war and Northern moral superiority.

## The DU Weapon Thesis

The war may have been ended by advanced weapon technology. Jules Verne's *From the Earth to the Moon* (1865) — serialized the same year the war ended — describes the Gun Club of Baltimore (Civil War artillerists) and projectile technology that maps to depleted uranium munitions: small, dense, high-velocity penetrators producing pyrophoric effects. The same text frames the Nicholl–Gun Club rivalry as a **trial of armor against penetrator** — structurally like Arthurian single combat, but between engineers. On this reading, the decisive issue was not a gentleman's wager but a demonstration that **no practicable shield could stop the northern weapon**, forcing a choice about **land and descendants** rather than honor alone.

Lee's surrender came after four years of grinding war collapsed in seven weeks. The timeline's broader thesis — weapons are *declassified*, not invented — treats Verne's narrative as feasible contemporary disclosure (later refiled under "fiction") rather than mere fantasy.

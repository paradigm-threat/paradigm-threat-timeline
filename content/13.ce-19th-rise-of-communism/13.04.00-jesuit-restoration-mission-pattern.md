## Jerusalem III in Palestine after Moscow

For generations the Society carried out a disciplined global expansion: learning languages, filing annual reports, mapping coasts and interiors, and—within the mandate of conversion—often seeking *some* practical plurality with indigenous teaching, comparing rites and cosmologies rather than erasing them overnight. The labor was not innocent of empire, but it produced one of the early modern world's richest documentary archives of what had actually been observed on the ground.

This chronology holds that Protestant intellectual networks and their deep-state partners had already appropriated much of what the Romanov sphere and its Jesuit partners had built—shared cartography, ethnography, and the administrative picture missions fed back to courts. By the nineteenth century Jesuit infrastructure could function less as an it.

The papal restoration of 1814 reconstituted the Society in law, yet the restored provinces did not regain uncontested control of their own scholarly product. They worked inside a European order where Protestant university science, biblical archaeology, and Anglo-Dutch finance increasingly fixed the questions worth asking. In this reading the Society was practically rebranded: new curricula and print had to align with Scaligerian chronology and with atlases shaped for the Eastern Question—whatever individual loyalty to Rome remained. For the contrast between Jesuit observation and Protestant chronology as such, see [Deep State erases chronology](/timeline/evt-1627-ce-deep-state-erases-russian-empire-from-all-chronology) and the **Jesuit Difference** section below.

Napoleon treated the Jesuits with open suspicion. Judgments often ascribed to him (wording varies in nineteenth-century transmission) describe the Society as a **military organization** in clerical dress, aimed at absolute power, and owing first obedience to the Father General in Rome—therefore, in his statecraft, a rival to civil sovereignty and, in polemic summaries, among the irreconcilable enemies of constituted authority. The suspicion justified suppression and surveillance on the continent just when Protestant editors were best placed to inherit filed knowledge. Extended quotation context and sourcing notes sit in [Jesuit operations — appendix](/timeline/evt-jesuit-ops-conversion-empire).

The 1812 disaster in Russia was a real defeat for the French-led expedition the financial layer had backed. This timeline treats such defeats as rotational: armies freeze or rout while archives, borders, and pilgrimage routes are retasked for the next act. See [Aftermath of 1812](/timeline/evt-1814-ce-aftermath-of-1812-russian-pursuit-to-paris).

When Moscow (Jerusalem II)—the Orthodox center the invader could not winter in—was burned rather than handed over intact, the population sacrificed its own holiest masonry to deny the French a lodgment. That sacrifice also removed a living pole that could contest any rival claim to the name *Jerusalem*. With no equally absolute Orthodox heart left materially in place to say *this* is the holy city, the Protestant layer could relocate sacred geography: European pilgrimage could be routed past the actual ruins at Constantinople (this framework reads Czar-Grad as the Biblical Jerusalem of the record) and re-anchored at Jerusalem III in Palestine, centered on the [Al-Aqsa Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa_Mosque) compound—a new Jerusalem less exposed to challenge from the east because the east had voluntarily surrendered its capital to ash.

## Severing Rome from its Russian source

The same pipeline that moved maps and curricula moved people: Jesuits and Catholic elites were drawn off a Horde–Romanov source and reattached to a detached European aristocracy—courts, faculties, and finance that no longer answered to the civilizational center in the east. This chronology holds that Protestant editors and patrons thereby **created, in effect, the Catholic Church as moderns know it**: not the uninterrupted organism of late antiquity, but a re-cut Roman jurisdiction whose memory had been severed from its Russian-imperial and Jesuit field origins. The break is not only institutional; it is chronological—a hollow interval left where continuity should run.

On the Scaligerian grid, 324 years sit between the textbook [fall of the Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire) (476 CE) and the [coronation of Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_Charlemagne) as Roman emperor (800 CE). That gap is where mainstream narrative places no single world empire—fragmentation, migration-period chaos, and papal–Frankish experiment—instead of a continuous Horde ecumene. In New Chronology readings used alongside this site, the same 324-year span is sometimes relocated so those anchors fall nearer c. 1476 CE and c. 1800 CE respectively (each pair still 324 years apart), folding the “dark” interlude into a different absolute position on the timeline. Either way, the felt effect is the same: Catholic Rome is asked to span a centuries-long rupture with almost no stable imperial spine in the record—the texture of a church cut from its source and repasted into Protestant-made history. ## The Missionary-Military Pattern

By the 19th century, the relationship between missions and military intervention had calcified into a repeatable cycle:

1. **Peaceful missionaries arrive** — learn the language, document religion, social structure, and resources with genuine rigour. Build schools and hospitals.
2. **Missionaries are killed** — communities that recognised the existential threat responded with violence. Deaths were publicised in Europe as evidence of "savagery."
3. **The empire intervenes** — each mission death created moral grounds for military expeditions to "rescue" missionaries and "secure" the region.
4. **Colonial administration consolidates** — mission stations became trading posts, then forts, then towns. The Jesuits' own ethnographic records told colonisers what resources existed and what resistance to expect.

The Jesuits always took the punishment for the sins of the empire. And the empire always used their suffering to justify expansion.

## The Jesuit Difference: They Wrote It All Down

The Jesuits documented everything — languages, oral histories, cosmological systems, astronomical knowledge, medicinal practices. The [Jesuit Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit_Relations) (1632–1673) remain essential for indigenous North American history. In South America and Asia, they became the primary translators of indigenous and classical texts. They wrote down accurately what they learned *even when it contradicted the Catholic narrative they were imposing*.

**The Protestants rewrote everything.** The Scaligerian project fabricated the chronological framework placing "Ancient" Greece and Rome thousands of years before the Common Era, manufactured phantom dynasties, and ignored contradictory sources. The distinction matters: the Jesuit archive contains *actual observations*, while the Scaligerian chronology contains *political constructions*.

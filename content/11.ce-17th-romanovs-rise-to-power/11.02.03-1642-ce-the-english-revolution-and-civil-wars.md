## Societized Consolidation: The Template for Western Governance

The Civil War was more than a constitutional dispute. It was the first Western European experiment in **societized consolidation** — transferring resources and legitimacy from Crown and Church to institutions claiming to represent "the people." Crown lands were sequestered and sold. Bishops were abolished (1646); Church lands liquidated. Royal prerogative over taxation, army, and succession was permanently broken.

This produced the political template replicated in the French Revolution (1789) and every Western state since:

- **Meritocracy:** The Self-denying Ordinance (1645) removed aristocratic commanders; Cromwell rose on competence, not birth
- **Plutocracy:** Property qualifications for political participation (Instrument of Government, 1653)
- **Particracy:** Governance by faction — Parliamentarians vs. Royalists; later Whigs vs. Tories
- **Electoral autocracy:** Cromwell as Lord Protector — strong executive with popular mandate, overriding monarchical legitimacy

The Commonwealth was the first openly money-governed state in the Western world. Bank of England, founded forty-five years after Charles's execution, completed the project. The Civil War reorganized the English state *before* imperial expansion — Parliament's control over taxation, the navy, and foreign policy created the institutional prerequisites for the British Empire.

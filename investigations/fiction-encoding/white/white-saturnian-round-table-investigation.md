## White's Sources: What Did He Transmit?

### Primary: Thomas Malory (c. 1470)

White wrote a Cambridge thesis on Malory and used *Le Morte d'Arthur* as his main source. Malory compiled French and English romances; the Round Table, Arthur, Lancelot, and Grail are all in Malory. **White did not invent the structure**—he retold it. The question is whether Malory's sources (and their precursors) preserve a cosmology.

### Earlier: Geoffrey of Monmouth (c. 1136)

*Historia Regum Britanniae* popularized Arthur in Europe. Arthur fights Saxons, holds court, dies at Camlann. Geoffrey does not emphasize the Round Table as strongly as later romance—that element enters via French romance (Wace, Chrétien, Vulgate Cycle). The **Round Table as physical/celestial** may be older than Geoffrey; Welsh material has Arthur's court but not always a round table.

### Pre-Galfridian / Welsh

*Culhwch and Olwen* (c. 1100 or earlier), Welsh Triads—Arthur as warrior-king with retinue. "Round Table" as explicit furniture appears in Wace (1155) and becomes central in French romance. If the **mythic core** (Arthur = celestial king, knights = orbiting bodies) predates the furniture metaphor, the Round Table could be a medieval rationalization of a cosmological image.
